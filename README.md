# Lunar Lander

A minimal 2D Lunar Lander built in **Godot 4.7.2** with GDScript, targeting **browser (Web) export**.

This repo is a playground for the whole game-making process: trying mechanics and gameplay, then building, deploying, publishing, and running the game live. Features are added in small, playable increments. This README is the shared context for AI agents generating new features; keep it current when a rule, convention, or constraint changes. Commit messages, PR titles, versioning and releases follow `CLAUDE.md`.

## Current state

Every run opens on the Office, a retro green-on-black Terminal where the player picks a ship type, browses Transport offers and accepts them as Contracts and starts loading them, and checks the Bank account. Contracts are flown down to the pad, unloaded and paid out into the Bank account. Then a scrolling two-area world: a surface with gravity, mountains and a landing pad, at one of three locations (the Valley, the Mine, an open-pit mine, or the Metropolis, a city with its pad on a rooftop), and gravity-free deep space with a maze-like station to dock in. Fuel, ship weight (with a Load that counts toward it), and refuelling on the pad and at the station. Domain terms are defined in `GLOSSARY.md`. Not yet set up: Web export preset, deployment, publishing, any live/production tooling.

## Gameplay rules

Tuning values are constants at the top of each script: world layout in `world.gd`, station shape in `station.gd`, per-ship values in `ship_type.gd`, rules shared by every ship type in `lander.gd`, camera in `camera.gd`. Those files are the source of truth for the numbers.

- **World:** a 2000 px wide column. From the bottom: the ground, the surface (with gravity), the border ~2270 px above the pad, deep space (no gravity, ~2600 px tall), and the station, which spans the full width and forms the ceiling. The surface's sky, and how dark its ground and mountains look, follow the local time (see Sky); deep space is black at every hour. The surface shows the current location: the Valley (flat ground between the mountains, the pad in the middle) or the Mine, an open-pit mine cut into the ground: from the Valley's ground level, benches step down (60 px steps, three a side, wider on the left) to a 600 px pit floor 180 px below the Valley's ground, with the pad off-centre on the pit floor toward its right wall. The camera follows the surface down to the pit floor. The benches are solid: touching them crashes the ship, like the mountains. The Metropolis (rough shapes for now) is a city on a street 192 px below the Valley's ground: two tall towers on the left (the first against the left mountain), the pad's building right of centre with its roof at about mid-height (300 px above the street), a thin solid antenna on that roof's left corner, and two lower roofs stepping down on its right to the right mountain, the approach. Only a 140 px stretch of the pad building's roof around the pad is ground; touching down on the rest of that roof, any building, the antenna or the street crashes the ship. Gaps between buildings are at least ~2.5× the Mule's length (180 px and more). The camera follows down to the street. The mountains, deep space and station are shared, and each location has its own Backdrop (the Valley the Tatra-like ridges, the Mine Himalaya-like peaks and its far walls, the Metropolis a skyline); the ground is too, at the location's height and width: by default it spans the whole world, but a location can narrow it to a landing surface centred on its pad (beside it the ship falls past its height onto whatever terrain is below, which crashes it). The camera's bottom limit, and the sky's ground colour filling below the Valley's ground, follow the location's lowest point (by default its ground's bottom, so the pit floor at the Mine). The location changes when a mission starts (see Loading) and by drifting (see Location drift); the planet clock owns it, and the World builds that location's pad, terrain and Backdrop each time it changes. The Mine's and the Metropolis's geometry is in `location.gd`.
- **Camera:** follows the lander with smoothing and stays inside the world. Zoom is 1.0 at ≤ 50 px/s, easing out to 0.65 at ≥ 400 px/s.

- **Controls:** in flight, Left/Right rotate, Up thrusts along the lander's nose (both ignored for a moment after a border crossing, see Border), R restarts in deep space (any time, including after a crash; in debug builds, R on the Surface only puts the ship back on the pad, see Restart). In the Office, Left/Right (and Up/Down) move between the main screen's tiles, Left/Right browse Ship offers or move between popup buttons, Up/Down move between Transport offers and their ACCEPT buttons, or between Contracts, DESTINATION, START PERIOD and START LOADING, Right/Left move between the Transport offers screen's columns, Return opens, selects or confirms (or cycles DESTINATION's or START PERIOD's value), Escape counts as No in a popup, closes the Transport offer under the cursor, returns to the main screen from any other screen, and closes the Office from the main screen. Arrows, Return and Escape use Godot's built-in `ui_*` actions; `restart` is a custom action bound by physical key.
- **Office:** every run opens on the Office: a full-window Terminal (black, green outlines and text in the Silkscreen pixel font, a border, scanlines) with the header `ORBITAL FREIGHT CO. // ` plus the screen's name, and a key hint for the screen at the bottom (arrows in a hint are drawn, Silkscreen has no arrow glyphs). The game's version (e.g. `V0.1.0`) sits dimly at the right end of the key hint row.
  - **Main screen** (`// OFFICE`): every opening of the Office lands here. Three tiles in a row, SHIP SELECTION, TRANSPORT OFFERS and BANK ACCOUNT; the selected one is inverted, and movement stops at both ends. Return opens a tile; Escape on any other screen comes back here with the same tile selected. Escape here powers the Terminal off and leaves the current ship Docked (the Sparrow at run start).
  - **Ship selection** opens on the ship type in service (the one in the parking spot), tagged IN SERVICE. It shows one Ship offer at a time: name, flavour line, a wireframe picture of the hull, TANK in kg, SEATS, CARGO (the full-Tank cargo with no passengers) and MAX CAPACITY (the maximum capacity) in kg, THRUST and HANDLING as 5-block bars, and a `< n/N >` indicator. Browsing wraps at both ends. Return on another Ship offer opens "ARE YOU SURE?" (Yes selected; the selected button is inverted). Yes puts that ship type Docked in the parking spot with a full Tank and its default dry mass, and returns to the main screen; No or Escape returns to the same Ship offer. Return on the IN SERVICE Ship offer just returns to the main screen.
  - **Transport offers** lists the Transport offers (new ones arrive only while fewer than 7 are listed; a removed Contract can take it past 7) in its left column and the Contracts in its right one. Each row shows its number (`#001`, numbered through the run), title, destination (`VALLEY`, `MINE` or `METROPOLIS`), PASSENGERS, CARGO or PASSENGERS + CARGO, and the time left to accept it as an in-game countdown, a started hour or minute counting as a whole one: hours (`20H` … `2H`), then in-game minutes for its last in-game hour, 30 real seconds (`60M` … `1M`); it's white, red in that last hour (black on the selected row, dimmed with the rest of an offer the ship can't take). Rows alternate between two faint green tints. Return on a row opens the offer's details under it (passengers, cargo, deadline as an in-game duration (`DEADLINE 3H 00M`), trader, value and destination (`DESTINATION MINE`), labels green and values white, with an ACCEPT button at their bottom right), on a slightly stronger tint, growing open over ~0.15 s (what isn't uncovered yet isn't drawn), and Return again closes them, shrinking them back the same way (the offer counts as closed at once: its ACCEPT is no longer a stop, and reopening grows the details from where they were); several offers can be open at once, and opening or closing one leaves the others. One cursor moves with Up/Down (stopping at both ends) through each offer's row, followed by its ACCEPT when it's open; the selected row or ACCEPT is inverted. Opening or closing an offer leaves the cursor on its row. When the list is taller than its area it scrolls just enough to keep the cursor's row or ACCEPT in view (on an open offer whose ACCEPT is dimmed, the row with its details, as much as fits), and small drawn ▲/▼ markers just right of the list's top and bottom show when more lies above or below; rows and lines that don't fit wholly in the area aren't drawn. The tints, animation time and marker size are constants in `office.gd` and `expandable_list.gd`. Escape on an open offer's row or ACCEPT closes that offer and puts the cursor on its row; anywhere else it returns to the main screen. An offer the ship can't take (counting the Contracts) has its text dimmed (row, reason and details; the cursor's highlight stays bright on it), with `OTHER DESTINATION` (while there are Contracts, it goes elsewhere than theirs), `NOT ENOUGH PASSENGER SEATS` or `OVERLOADED` (its cargo would pass the maximum capacity) under its row, the first that applies, and its ACCEPT is dimmed and skipped by the cursor; fuel never disables an offer. An offer that fits but would, on top of the Contracts, leave room for less than a full Tank stays lit and acceptable, with a red `FUEL LIMITED` note under its row. Return on an ACCEPT asks "ARE YOU SURE?" (Yes selected) for that offer only; Yes makes that offer a Contract, which leaves the list (nothing takes its place at once). Confirming checks again that the offer is still there and still fits, its destination included. An open offer that expires just closes; if its ARE YOU SURE? was showing, that closes too, nothing is accepted and `OFFER EXPIRED` shows at the bottom left for 2 s. The right column lists CONTRACTS (`#006 TITLE`), then `PASSENGERS X/Y`, `CARGO X/Y KG` (accepted cargo over the full-Tank cargo for the accepted passengers), `FUEL LIMITED: X/Y KG` when the Contracts' Load leaves room for less than a full Tank (the fuel cap over the Tank), `DELIVER WITHIN 2H 30M` (the shortest Deadline among the Contracts, as an in-game duration: 2 in-game minutes per real second) and `TOTAL VALUE €N` (the sum of their rewards), both hidden while there are no Contracts, then the mission rows, `DESTINATION VALLEY` and `START PERIOD EVENING` (green labels, white values; a whole row inverted while it holds the cursor), and a START LOADING button; Return on it starts loading (see Loading). Without Contracts it reads START instead and starts a free flight (see Free flight). DESTINATION shows a location; Return on it cycles Valley → Mine → Metropolis → Valley. Accepting the first Contract sets it to that Contract's destination and locks it: while there are Contracts, Return on it does nothing. Removing the last Contract unlocks it, keeping its value. START PERIOD shows Morning, Day, Evening or Night; Return on it cycles Morning → Day → Evening → Night → Morning. Each time the Office opens, they start on the Contracts' destination (else the current location) and the start period holding the current local time. They work without Contracts too, but only START LOADING (or START) applies them: changing them without loading leaves the location and the local time alone. Summary labels are green and values white; once the cargo passes the full-Tank cargo the CARGO value turns red, and FUEL LIMITED shows, all red, at exactly that moment. Cargo beyond the full-Tank cargo, up to the maximum capacity, can still be accepted. Right moves focus to the Contracts column, on START LOADING (open offers stay open; the list's row is then outlined), Left moves it back. There, Up/Down move through the Contracts, then DESTINATION, then START PERIOD, then START LOADING (stopping at both ends); each Contract has a plain `X` at the right end of its row, on the same baseline as its text, and the selected Contract's whole row is inverted, and START LOADING is inverted only while it holds the cursor. Return on a Contract asks "REMOVE CONTRACT?" (No selected); No or Escape keeps it, Yes removes it: no payout, no transaction, its seats and cargo are freed, and it goes back to the offers with the same number, in number order (closed), with the time left it had when accepted (an offer's time doesn't run while it's a Contract), even when 7 or more are listed; it keeps its destination. The cursor then stays on a Contract while any are left, otherwise on START LOADING. Contracts stay accepted when offers expire and when the player leaves the Office; once loading starts they can't be removed (Return on one does nothing). During a loaded mission (from the end of loading until unloading starts) every offer is dimmed with `MISSION UNDER WAY` and can't be accepted, and the button reads CANCEL MISSION: Return on it asks "CANCEL MISSION?" (No selected); No or Escape keeps the mission, Yes cancels it (see Cancelling) and the cursor stays on the button, which then reads START.

  Transport offers come from the Economy. A run starts with 5. While fewer than 7 are listed, a new one arrives after a random 30–90 s (the wait pauses while 7 are listed and is drawn afresh when it runs again). Each one expires 180–600 s after it appears (offer timers run everywhere, Office open or not). Offers leave the pool only by expiring or being accepted, and neither brings a new one at once. Each offer's destination is drawn first, by the locations' pool shares (Valley 40%, Mine 30%, Metropolis 30%; fixed when it appears), and the rest comes from that destination's location profile: its kind weights (passengers only / cargo only / mixed), passenger and cargo ranges (in 100 kg steps, each capped by the largest ship type's seats and maximum capacity, so some won't fit a smaller ship; nothing makes sure one fits), titles and reward factor. The Valley: ⅓ / ⅓ / ⅓, 1–10 passengers, 500 kg to the maximum, ×1.0, with varied passengers, tourists, luggage, supplies and cargo. The Mine: 10% / 60% / 30%, 1–4 passengers, 3 000 kg to the maximum, ×1.6, with tools, parts, industrial supplies and small crews. The Metropolis: 60% / 10% / 30%, 1–10 passengers, 500–3 000 kg, ×1.35, mostly tourists and corporate workers, with occasional light cargo, mail and shop supplies. Deadline 60–180 s everywhere; reward €150 per passenger and €60 per 1 000 kg of cargo at a 120 s deadline, × (120 / deadline)^0.5, × a random 0.7–1.3, × the destination's reward factor. On average a Mine offer pays about a fifth more than a Valley one (heavy and cargo-led, so it needs the larger factor), and a Metropolis offer a little less than a Mine one (about €910, €1 100 and €1 085). The pool sizes and arrival interval live in `economy.gd`, the location profiles in `location.gd`, the other numbers in `transport_offer.gd`.
  - **Bank account** shows `PILOT` and `BALANCE €0` (label green, amount white): the balance in EUR, held by the Economy node and starting at €0 every run (a negative one reads `-€120`), then, a row's height below, one transaction per delivered or cancelled Contract, newest first, its row showing only the payout: `#004 INDUSTRIAL SUPPLIES  CARGO  €544`. A Contract that paid €0 still gets one. The transactions work like the Transport offer list (`ExpandableList`), without buttons: alternating row tints, Up/Down move the cursor (stopping at both ends, the selected row inverted), Return opens or closes the transaction's details on the stronger tint with the same opening and closing animation, several can be open at once, the list scrolls to keep the cursor's row in view, with its details when it's open (as much as fits) with ▲/▼ markers, and Escape on an open transaction closes it (anywhere else it returns to the main screen). Open details show, labels green and values white: `BASE VALUE €640`, `PENALTY -€96 (24 MIN OVERDUE)` (Silkscreen has no `−` glyph, so it's a hyphen; the whole real seconds overdue the euros were counted for, as in-game minutes: 2 per second) or `PENALTY €0` when delivered on time, `PAYOUT €544`, `DEADLINE 14:30` (the flight's deadline the penalty was counted against, as a local time: the shortest among its Contracts, which can be shorter than this Contract's own), `LANDED 14:54` (the local time of landing on the pad), `DELIVERED TO VALLEY` (the location landed at) and `SHIP SPARROW` (the ship type in service when it was settled). A cancelled Contract (see Cancelling) shows `BASE VALUE €420`, `CANCELLATION FEE -€105`, `PAYOUT -€105`, `CANCELLED` and `SHIP SPARROW`, without the deadline, landing and delivered-to lines. A Contract unloaded on a pad that isn't its destination (see Payout) shows `WRONG DESTINATION -€640` in place of the PENALTY line, `PAYOUT €0`, and `DESTINATION MINE` after DELIVERED TO. Times on the day after loading finished end in ` +1` (`LANDED 00:15 +1`).

  The world is paused while the Office is showing. The Terminal powers on with a ~0.3 s CRT effect (a dot stretches into a bright line, which opens into the picture) and powers off with the reverse; the flight view shows only once power-off is over, and Office input is ignored during the effect. The "ENTER THE OFFICE?" popup over the flight view opens and closes without it.
- **Start:** once the player leaves the Office, the lander (the Sparrow unless another ship type was chosen) is Docked in the centre of the station's parking spot (re-picked at random each run), facing up, at rest, with a full tank. The pad is a destination only: fly out of the maze and down to the surface to land there.
- **Ship types:** each ship type has its own hull, flame, default dry mass, Maximum weight, Tank, passenger seats, cargo capacity, thrust, fuel burn and turn rate. Thrust and burn are given at the reference mass. The Sparrow: 10 000 kg default dry mass, 22 000 kg Maximum weight, 5 000 kg Tank, 4 seats, 8 000 kg cargo capacity, 150 px/s² thrust, 250 kg/s burn, 3.0 rad/s turn rate. The Halo: a C-shaped ring 40 px across with a 16 px hole, open at the nose (concave hull, gap included in the collision shape), flame at the back; 14 000 kg default dry mass, 32 000 kg Maximum weight, 8 000 kg Tank, 10 seats, 11 000 kg cargo capacity, 214 px/s² thrust, 231 kg/s burn, 2.2 rad/s turn rate (climbs off the pad at ~80% of the Sparrow's rate, and a full tank lasts ~1.2× as long as the Sparrow's). The Mule: a 42×68 px box with its two nose corners rounded to 4 px (back corners square), long axis along the thrust, flame on the short side at the back; 20 000 kg default dry mass, 41 000 kg Maximum weight, 12 000 kg Tank, 4 seats, 16 000 kg cargo capacity, 316 px/s² thrust, 207 kg/s burn, 1.5 rad/s turn rate (climbs off the pad at ~60% of the Sparrow's rate, and a full tank lasts ~1.4× as long as the Sparrow's). Each Maximum weight is the highest total at which that ship type still hovers and reaches the border crossing speed (`tests/test_*.gd` check this by adding a Load that brings a full ship to it). At full seats and cargo, every ship type has room for less than a full Tank. Each ship type's cargo capacity is its maximum capacity, the hard limit; its full-Tank cargo, the most cargo that still leaves room for a full Tank, is Maximum weight − dry mass − Tank − passengers × 80 kg, kept within 0 and the maximum capacity: Sparrow 7 000 kg, Halo 10 000 kg, Mule 9 000 kg with no passengers. Thrust-time targets are ratios to the Sparrow's measured full-tank thrust time (~24 s; its nominal Tank / burn is 20 s), not absolute seconds; `tests/test_halo.gd` and `tests/test_mule.gd` check them that way.
- **Mass:** total mass = dry mass (always the ship type's default) + fuel + Load (the passengers and cargo carried, 0 until loading finishes). Maximum weight caps the total: the fuel cap is min(Tank, Maximum weight − dry mass − Load), and refuelling, on the pad and at the station, stops there. The factor `w = total / 15 000 kg` divides thrust acceleration and scales gravity by half as much (`1 + 0.5·(w−1)`). Fuel burn is multiplied by its own factor, which counts the Load at half its mass: `(dry mass + fuel + 0.5·Load) / 15 000 kg`, so a loaded ship burns more gently than its weight alone would give, and a ship without a Load burns at `w`. Both factors change live as fuel burns or refills. Maximum weight is chosen so every ship type can still hover and cross the border.
- **Fuel:** thrust burns fuel (scaled by the burn factor above); a default full ship without a Load empties the tank in ~20 s of thrust. Burn is ×2 for the first 1 s after lifting off the pad (not after undocking), and up to ×2 more when thrusting against the motion (scaled by how directly, and by speed up to 150 px/s). At 0 fuel the engine doesn't fire.
- **Border:** gravity fades smoothly to zero over the 200 px below the border and is zero above it. Deep space flight is pure Newtonian: thrust is still divided by `w`, and there's no drag. Crossing the border either way needs ≥ 150 px/s vertical speed. A slower ship is sprung back to the side it came from. A real crossing shakes the screen for 2 s, starts steering until the ship is 150 px past the line, and disturbs the ship: for 0.6 s at 150 px/s down to 0.3 s at ≥ 400 px/s (by speed at the crossing), Left/Right and Up are ignored while the nose wobbles 4 times (each up to half the largest turn either way) and settles on a random lasting turn, either way, of up to 12° at 150 px/s down to 3° at ≥ 400 px/s (never under a third of that). The velocity turns with the nose, keeping its speed, so the path bends by the same lasting angle; nothing eases back. A ship sprung back by the border isn't disturbed. The numbers are the `DISTURB_*` constants in `lander.gd`.
- **Steering:** the game only damps the ship's speed and pushes it back; it never turns the ship. The nose stays where the player aimed it, and Left/Right and thrust work as in normal flight (apart from the border's disturbance). After a border crossing, speed is damped to 100 px/s.
- **Sides:** below the mountain tops, the mountains wall both sides and touching them crashes the ship. Above them, both sides are soft walls: within 150 px of an edge, sideways speed toward the edge is damped, the ship is pushed back inward, keeping its rotation, and it can never pass the edge.
- **Station:** a straight 100 px entry corridor leads from the station's bottom face into a vertical shaft. Three arms branch left and three right, like two back-to-back E letters. On each run, one of the six arm endings, picked at random, holds a chamber with the 120×136 px parking spot (cyan outline). Touching any station wall crashes the ship.
- **Docked** needs the whole ship inside the parking spot, at ≤ 40 px/s, while not thrusting. It then behaves like Landed: the ship zeroes velocity, eases upright, refuels after 2 s, and shows "Docked!". While docked, Left/Right turn the ship in place so it can face the way out. Up undocks it. The ship must leave the spot before it can dock again.
- **Loading:** START LOADING powers the Terminal off and starts loading over the flight view, starting the mission: the location becomes the chosen destination, the Contracts' (its pad and terrain replace the old ones while the ship is docked at the station, and the HUD shows its name, `MINE  14:07`), and the local time is set to a random whole minute within the chosen start period (Morning 06:00–11:59, Day 12:00–17:59, Evening 18:00–23:59, Night 00:00–05:59), re-rolled every time, even for the current period. The clock keeps running from there through loading, the delivery flight and unloading. Fuel above the fuel cap for the Contracts' Load is pumped out at once. The ship stays Docked and held (Up doesn't undock, Left/Right don't turn it, no cancelling) while the message counts down `Loading… 7s`; loading takes 3 s + 1 s per passenger + 1 s per 1 000 kg of cargo. Meanwhile it refuels as when resting, from the first frame (no 2 s delay), up to that cap, with `Refueling…` under the countdown. When it finishes, the Load is added to the ship's mass, the hold is released (refuelling carries on up to the cap), and the message shows `Loaded! Deliver by 14:30` (the flight deadline as a local time, see HUD), plus `Fuel limited to 3 680 kg` when the cap is below a full Tank, until the ship undocks. The delivery clock starts the moment loading finishes, even if the ship stays Docked; its Deadline is the shortest among the Contracts. Docking again while loaded asks "ENTER THE OFFICE?" as usual, so the mission can be cancelled; no Contracts can be added.
- **Cancelling:** CANCEL MISSION in the Office, during a loaded mission, gives up every Contract at once. Each one is charged a cancellation fee of 25% of its reward (rounded), recorded as a transaction with a negative payout (`-€105`) and taken from the balance, which may go negative. The Load comes off at once (the fuel cap is a full Tank again), the mission ends (no HUD deadline, no delivery clock), and accepting offers and ship swap unlock. Cancelled Contracts don't go back to the Transport offers.
- **Free flight:** with no Contracts the button reads START. It powers the Terminal off and starts the mission like START LOADING (the location becomes the chosen destination, the local time a random whole minute within the chosen start period), but nothing is loaded: the ship stays Docked and is released, so Up undocks it at once, there's no fuel cap, no deadline in the HUD and no delivery clock. Landing on any pad is a plain Landed with no unloading, and docking again asks "ENTER THE OFFICE?" as usual.
- **Unloading:** becoming Landed on the current location's pad while delivering (a real landing or the debug pad shortcut) starts unloading by itself, whether or not it's the Contracts' destination (drift may have taken that away, see Location drift); the delivery clock stops and the HUD deadline goes. The message counts down `Unloading… 4s` (2 s + 0.5 s per passenger + 0.5 s per 1 000 kg of cargo). Lift-off is blocked (Up does nothing), but refuelling goes on as usual, up to the fuel cap while the Load is aboard. Only the first landing counts: a ship that settles back, or lands again, doesn't unload twice.
- **Payout:** when unloading finishes, each Contract is settled on its own for the whole seconds overdue at landing (rounded down, like the red counter): it loses 2% of its passenger reward and 1% of its cargo reward per second, each part never below €0. Unloaded on a pad that isn't its destination, it pays €0 instead (its whole reward is lost, however late). The total goes into the Bank account, one transaction per Contract is added, and a small green `+€420` rises above the ship and fades over 2 s. The Load and the Contracts are cleared, the fuel cap is a full Tank again (refuelling carries on), and the ship can lift off.
- **Ship swap:** every time the ship docks (not at run start, not on the pad, not while loading; while delivering too), a popup over the flight view asks "ENTER THE OFFICE?" with No selected, once the ship has finished easing upright (undocking before then cancels it). From the moment the popup shows until the ship is released it's frozen: Up doesn't undock, Left/Right don't turn it, and the refuel timer doesn't run; R still restarts (the station is in deep space). No or Escape closes the popup and refuelling starts after the usual 2 s. Yes opens the Office on the main screen. Choosing another ship type in Ship selection puts it Docked in the same parking spot; the station isn't rebuilt. While there are Contracts, ship swapping is locked: Ship offers other than the one in service show `SHIP LOCKED: CONTRACTS ACCEPTED` and Return on them does nothing (browsing still works). Leaving the Office (Escape on the main screen) releases the ship as No would, whether or not it was swapped.
- **Judged on every touchdown:** the result is decided the moment the lander touches the ground. Nothing can save a bounce.
- **Landed** needs all of these at touchdown:
  - vertical speed within its limit
  - horizontal speed within its limit
  - tilt from upright within its limit (angle is wrapped, so any number of full spins doesn't count)
  - lander center within the pad
- **Crashed** is any other touchdown (including the Mine's pit floor off the pad, or the Metropolis's pad roof beside the pad), or touching the mountains, a location's terrain (the Mine's benches and its crane; the Metropolis's buildings, antenna and street) or the station.
- **Landed:** the lander turns green, zeroes its velocity, eases upright over 0.3 s and rests (no gravity, no rotation). After 2 s it refuels at half a Tank per 6 s up to the fuel cap, showing "Refueling…". Up lifts off at any time (except while unloading), cancelling refuelling. A ship too heavy to climb just settles back, which counts as another landing.
- **Crashed:** physics stops, the lander turns red, and the label shows "Crashed!" plus "Press R to retry". A crash is final.
- **HUD:** right edge, a vertical fuel bar showing how full the current ship type's Tank is, with a brown, wavy, eased fill (frame flashes red when empty), "FUEL" above, total mass in kg below. In the upper right, always (flying, Docked, Landed or Crashed, on the Surface or in deep space), the clock line: the current location's name and the local time, `VALLEY  14:07` (HH:MM, no seconds). Under it, while delivering, the destination and the flight deadline as a local time, `MINE BY 14:30` (the clock line above still shows the current location): the local time when loading finished plus the flight deadline × 2 in-game minutes per real second, wrapped at midnight, with no day mark (a next-day deadline reads `MINE BY 01:10`). Once the flight deadline passes it turns into a red `MINE OVERDUE SINCE 14:30`. Only the presentation is in local time: the deadline and penalties still count real seconds from the end of loading.
- **Local time:** one planet clock, in in-game minutes, advancing smoothly at 120× real time: 1 real second is 2 in-game minutes, 30 s an hour, a whole day 12 real minutes; 23:59 wraps to 00:00. It runs whenever the world does (flying, Docked, loading, Landed, unloading, Crashed) and stops while the Office shows (it pauses with the tree). A run starts at the Valley at a random whole minute between 18:00 and 23:59 (the Evening start period). See `docs/adr/0002-one-planet-clock.md`.
- **Location drift:** the planet turns under the station. A hidden count adds in-game time while the ship is above the Border, in any state (flying, Docked, held, loading, Crashed). Going below the Border starts it over, so time on the surface never counts, and so does every location change. At 48 in-game hours (24 real minutes) the location becomes the next one in the location table (Valley → Mine → Metropolis → Valley…), in any phase, including during a delivery: landing on the new location's pad unloads the Load, but the Contracts pay €0 unless it's their destination. The change waits while any part of the surface (anything below the Border) is inside the camera's visible area, and happens on the first frame it isn't, so the surface never visibly swaps; the HUD name changes in the same frame as the pad and terrain. The local time carries on unchanged. The count stops while the Office shows (it pauses with the tree). See `docs/adr/0002-one-planet-clock.md`.
- **Restart** reloads the whole scene, so every run starts from the scene's initial state: back on the Office main screen with the Sparrow, a €0 balance, a new Transport offer pool numbered from `#001`, the parking spot re-picked, and the planet clock back at the Valley at a new random Evening minute. In debug builds (a testing shortcut), R on the Surface (below the border, whether flying, Landed or Crashed) instead puts the ship Landed on the current location's pad, upright and at rest, keeping its ship type, dry mass, fuel and Load; the crash clears. In deep space, and always in release builds, R restarts.
- **Backdrop:** each location's own scenery behind its surface, with no collision, built when the World shows that location. The Metropolis shows a far skyline in two depths, flat roofs and straight walls of distant towers below the mountain tops, each depth its own parallax layer, filled like the ridges. The Valley shows two Tatra-like ridges: a farther, jagged High Tatra ridge peaking at the mountain tops near Rysy, and a nearer, rounded ridge with Giewont's sleeping knight. The Mine shows three layers: tall, jagged Himalaya-like peaks towering above the mountain tops, lower sharp foothills, and its far walls, terraced down well below the benches so the pit looks deep, with a headframe, a chimney and two cranes on them; its layers move more slowly than the Tatras (stronger parallax). Only the crane over the Mine's left benches is solid (terrain); the Backdrop's cranes have no collider. Each is filled with the background gradient, darkened (`TATRA_FAR_DARKENING`, `TATRA_NEAR_DARKENING` in `backdrop.gd`), down to the location's lowest point. Each ridge is its own parallax layer, moving more slowly than the world, the farther one more so (`TATRA_FAR_SCROLL`, `TATRA_NEAR_SCROLL`): mostly sideways, with only a small vertical factor so they barely rise into the sky as the ship climbs. Every parallax layer (Backdrop, clouds, Foreground) is anchored on the pad's top: with the camera centred there it sits exactly where it was designed. The mountains, ground and terrain stay fixed in the world, and the mountains cover the ridges' ends. Since the ridges derive from the gradient, they follow the sky.
- **Clouds:** the Valley has a few clouds high in its sky, in a far parallax layer, drifting slowly rightwards on the wind by themselves and wrapping around from one side to the other out of sight. Each is the sky colour behind it, lightened by day and darkened with the sky at night (orange in the evening). No collider.
- **Foreground:** silhouettes in front of the ship, moving faster than the world, which can briefly hide the ship and its path (Hollow Knight style): at the Valley two stands of Tatra spruces near the mountains' feet, well to the sides of the pad; at the Mine, moving faster still (stronger parallax), a spoil heap and a haul truck on the left benches, an ore heap on the pit floor left of the pad and an ore heap with a conveyor's head tower on the right, all below the crane's arm so they never hide it; at the Metropolis two near rooftops, one with a water tank and a mast, one with a billboard, well to the sides of the pad's building. Opaque and near-black, darker than all other scenery at every hour and a little darker at night, with no lights and no collider. With the camera following the ship within ~250 px above the pad, no Foreground piece covers the pad, the ship or the edge of solid terrain there; a new location's Foreground must keep that.
- **City lights:** the Metropolis's buildings have lit windows, neon signs and red warning lights blinking on the towers' and the lower roof's corners and the antenna's tip, and a few distant vehicles fly across its sky behind the buildings, each with a light, wrapping around out of sight. The lights are never dimmed: faint by day, they grow brighter as the sky darkens, full at night, so the city reads at a glance in the dark. The buildings and the vehicles' hulls dim with the scenery. None of it has a collider.
- **Sky:** the sky follows the local time. Its ground-level colour blends smoothly (no jumps at hour boundaries, wrapping around midnight) between keyframes: 03:00 dark navy, 07:30 pale yellow, 13:00 blue, 19:00 today's surface orange (exactly), 23:00 dark navy (so it stays navy through midnight). The gradient still fades from it to black at the Border, so deep space looks the same at every hour. The ground, mountains and a location's terrain (the Mine's benches) are darkened by the sky's darkness, most at night and not at all by day or at 19:00; the pad never is, so it stays easy to find. The pad's Beacon, a small light on each of its edges, pulses slowly (never going out) and is never dimmed either; its soft glow is faint by day and grows with the sky's darkness, strongest at night. It has no collider: the ship flies through it. Faint night stars cover the whole surface sky, from the Border down to the ground, drawn behind the Backdrop (the ridges, ground and mountains cover them) at the sky's star brightness: none by day, fading in 20:30–22:30 and out 05:00–07:00, capped faint. The deep-space stars and their fade just below the Border are the same at every hour. Time changes nothing in gameplay. The keyframes are constants in `sky_look.gd`.
- There is no win state: the Bank account balance is the only measure of a run, and a crash ends it. You can fly between the station and the pad, docking and landing as often as you like.

Deliberately not built yet: a score or high-score table, HUD readouts (speed, altitude), terrain generation, sound, explosions, multiple levels.

## Constraints

- **Compatibility renderer only** (`gl_compatibility`). Godot's Web export supports only this renderer; keep it when changing project settings.
- **Godot-native nodes and GDScript only.** No external assets except bundled OFL fonts (see ADR 0001), and no addons or GDExtensions; shapes are drawn with `Polygon2D` or `_draw`.
- **Stay minimal:** the smallest change that makes a feature playable. Add a new scene or script only when one file genuinely stops being readable.
- **Read screen size at runtime** with `get_viewport_rect()`. The window uses `canvas_items` stretch with `expand` aspect, so the visible area changes with window shape.

## Code conventions

- Tuning values are `UPPER_SNAKE` constants at the top of the script, grouped by topic. The ship types (`ShipType.SPARROW`, `MULE`, `HALO`, `ALL`) are named like constants too, but are `static var`s because GDScript can't build objects in a `const`; never reassign them (tests may append a temporary entry to `ALL` and remove it again).
- Node references are `@onready var` with typed `$Path` lookups.
- Static typing everywhere (`:=`, typed parameters and return values).
- Comments only for non-obvious reasons (see the `move_and_slide()` note in `lander.gd`).

## Gotchas

- `move_and_slide()` removes the velocity pushing into the ground, so the impact velocity is saved *before* the call. Any new touchdown rule must read that saved value.
- Any collision with something other than `Ground` (mountains, a location's terrain, station) is a crash; only ground contacts go to landing judgement.
- The parking spot has no collider either; docking checks that every hull vertex is inside the spot's `Rect2`.
- The station's walls are the station block minus the union of passage rectangles (`Geometry2D`). The entry cut reaches below the block so that the result is one polygon with no holes. Keep passages overlapping when changing the layout.
- The pad has no collider; landing is judged against the ground collider, and the "on the pad" check compares x positions. The pad's position comes from the current location (`World.location.pad_center_x`, from the `Location` table), never a constant: the drawn pad, the "on the pad" check and the debug pad shortcut all read it. The planet clock owns the current location (`World.location` reads it); `PlanetClock.move_to()` emits `location_changed`, on which the World moves the drawn pad and rebuilds the `Terrain` body's children. A location sets its ground's height (`Location.ground_y`; the Mine's pit floor is below the Valley's ground) and width (`Location.ground_width`): the World moves the whole `Ground` body there (centred on the world at full width, on the pad when narrowed) and gives it a fresh `RectangleShape2D` and visual polygon of that width, so the ground collider, `ground_top()`, the pad (a child of `Ground`, placed relative to it) and landing judgement follow it. Only the ground collider is landed on, so a narrowed ground is the only place a ship can land there; everything else it can touch must be terrain (a crash). The camera's bottom limit and the sky fill below the Valley's ground read `World.lowest_point()` (`Location.lowest_y`), not the ground's bottom: set it on a location whose terrain reaches below its ground. Never read `GROUND_TOP_Y`/`GROUND_BOTTOM_Y` for where the ground is now; they are the Valley's. Its size is still `PAD_WIDTH`/`PAD_HEIGHT` in `lander.gd`. The pad only appears at runtime, because the World's `_ready()` builds its shape.
- `Station` must stay above `Lander` in `main.tscn`: siblings become ready in order, and the Lander's `_ready()` reads `station.parking_spot` to place the docked start.
- The Office pauses the whole tree (`get_tree().paused`) while showing; only the Office (process mode Always) handles input then. R is handled by the Lander, so it does nothing while the Office is showing. `SceneTree.paused` survives a scene reload; the new Office sets it again in `_ready()`. Anything that must keep running behind the Office (the Economy) needs process mode Always too. Keep economy state in scene nodes (no autoload, no static state), so a restart wipes it. The planet clock is the opposite of the Economy: it must stop behind the Office, so it keeps the default (pausable) process mode.
- The Lander's `_ready()` still makes it a Docked Sparrow behind the Office; choosing a Ship offer calls `start_docked(type)` again. `start_docked()` never asks to enter the Office; only `dock()` does, by emitting `docked_at_station` from the end of its level tween, so the signal waits for the ship to be upright and lifting off first (`enter()` kills the tween) cancels it. The Office answers with the "ENTER THE OFFICE?" popup and calls `lander.hold()`, which freezes `_physics_process`; the Lander doesn't know why it's held. No, and leaving the Office from the main screen, call `release()`; the refuel delay starts over because docking zeroed it and a held ship doesn't count it down. Choosing a ship type calls `start_docked(type)`, and entering Docked releases the hold. `start_loading()` releases it too: a loading ship isn't held but rests (so it refuels), with `loading` blocking lift-off and turning.
- The Lander's `state` (Flying, Landed, Docked, Crashed) changes only through `enter()`, which starts every state fresh: not held, no lift-off burn, docking armed, leveling stopped, with the state's colour and message. `start_docked()`, `reset_to_pad()` and `fly_from()` also move the ship and clear any border bounce, steering or disturbance. `enter()` also clears the `status` line. `rest()` rewrites the message every frame (title, `status`, then Refueling…), so outside text such as the loading and unloading countdowns must go through `show_status()`, which also shows it right away (keeping the Refueling… line) for a held ship (held ships don't rest). `lift_off_blocked` is set from outside like `held`, but `enter()` doesn't clear it, so a ship that settles back onto the pad while unloading stays grounded; the Economy clears it when unloading finishes. `lift_off()` is the only transition that looks at the state it leaves (the lift-off burn from the pad, no re-docking from the spot). Crashed and held make `_physics_process` return early; Landed and Docked skip gravity and judging. Restart is handled in `_unhandled_input`.
- The sky is one pure step (`SkyLook.at()`), and the World only draws from it: keep time-of-day looks there, never in the clock. Solid scenery dims through `self_modulate`, so its own `color` stays the day colour; a new solid visual (such as a location's terrain) joins with `World.add_scenery(visual)` once it's in the tree. Don't put the pad (or anything under `Ground` other than its visual) in the scenery group, and don't modulate `Ground` itself: the pad is its child.
- Parallax decor goes in an `AnchoredParallax` (a `Parallax2D`), never moved by hand from the camera: anchored on the current pad's top, it's in its designed place with the camera centred there. `World.shown_decor()` places a parallax piece with `offset_for()` for the camera it's given (not the actual one), so a new parallax piece is listed right by just living in such a layer. Decor that must not show an edge (Backdrop ridges) reaches far past the side overhang and the lowest point; nothing in a parallax layer has a collider. Moving decor (clouds) only changes node positions, never redraws.
- City windows are never drawn one by one either: they're one mesh per location, and the sky only sets the city lights' `modulate`; warning lights blink and vehicles fly by changing node `modulate` and positions, never redrawing.
- Stars are never drawn one by one: each star layer is one mesh built once, and the sky's star brightness only sets the night layer's `modulate`. Thousands of `draw_circle` calls cost ~8–13 ms every frame and a long hitch on every redraw, and the World redraws its gradient and the Backdrop once per in-game minute (every 0.5 s).
- Leveling after a landing lifts the corner that touched down, so the level tween ends with a `move_and_collide()` back onto the ground.

## Running and validating

Godot isn't on `PATH`; the editor binary is `~/Downloads/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot`.

- **Play:** open the project in the editor and press F5.
- **Import / parse check:** `Godot --headless --path . --import`
- **Smoke run** (prints any script errors): `Godot --headless --path . --quit-after 600`
- **Tests:** `Godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 -s tests/run_tests.gd` runs every test and exits non-zero if any fails. In a fresh checkout, run `--import` first. `--fixed-fps 60` makes each frame one physics step without waiting for real time; the tests count physics frames, so they give the same results without it, just slower.
  - The runner loads every `tests/test_*.gd` and calls each `test_*` method on a fresh main scene. A test fails if a `check` fails, if no checks ran, or if any script error is logged while it runs.
  - Every run opens on the Office, so a test that flies calls `await choose_ship()` (or `choose_ship(ShipType.MULE)` etc.) first: from the main screen it opens Ship selection with `open_ship_selection()`, browses to that Ship offer with `browse_to(type)`, presses Return and confirms Yes (the ship type in service needs no Yes), then presses Escape to leave the Office, leaving the ship Docked. Office tests read `office()`: `showing`, `screen` (`Office.Screen`), `tile_labels()`, `selected_tile()`, `popup_text()`, `selected_ship_offer()`, `ship_offer_lines()` (the Ship offer's text, ratings as ■/□ blocks), `ship_offer_picture()`, `in_service_shown()`, `popup` (`Office.PopupKind`, including `REMOVE_CONTRACT` and `CANCEL_MISSION`) and `selected_button` (`Office.Choice`). The Transport offers screen is `office().transport_offers`: `rows()`, `reasons()` (per row, `""` when the offer fits; `OVERLOADED` past the maximum capacity, `MISSION UNDER WAY` while delivering), `red_notes()` (per row, the red `FUEL LIMITED` note or `""`), `list` (its `ExpandableList`): `list.selected_index()` (the row under the cursor), `list.expanded_rows()` (the open rows, top to bottom), `list.scroll` (pixels the list is scrolled down), `list.more_above()` and `list.more_below()` (what the ▲/▼ markers show) and `list.cursor_in_view()` (the row or ACCEPT under the cursor lies wholly in the list's area); `details(row)` (that row's details, ending with ACCEPT; empty while closed), `on_accept()` (the cursor is on that row's ACCEPT), `notice()` (`OFFER EXPIRED` while it shows), `focus` (`TransportOffersScreen.Column`), `selected_contract()` (the Contract under the Contracts column's cursor, `null` on a mission row or START LOADING), `mission_lines()` (the mission rows, top to bottom, e.g. `DESTINATION MINE`, `START PERIOD MORNING`), `selected_mission_line()` (the label of the mission row under the cursor, e.g. `DESTINATION`, or `""`), `on_start_button()` (the Contracts column's cursor is on the start button: START, START LOADING or CANCEL MISSION), `contract_lines()`, `summary()`, `red_summary_lines()` (the summary lines whose value is red) and `start_text()` (`START LOADING`, `START` without Contracts, or `CANCEL MISSION` during a loaded mission); accepted offers are in `economy().contracts`. The Bank account screen is `office().bank_account`: `lines()` (`PILOT` and the balance), `rows()` (the transactions, newest first), `details(row)` (that transaction's breakdown; empty while closed) and `list`, read as for Transport offers; `open_bank_account()` in `scene_test.gd` opens it from the main screen. The delivery is `economy().phase` (`Economy.Phase`), `economy().loading_left`, `economy().delivery_time` (seconds since loading finished), `economy().deadline()`, `economy().delivery_left()`, `economy().loaded_at` (the local time when loading finished; a test may set it once loading has finished to fix the local times a transaction records), `economy().deadline_at()` and `economy().landed_at` (the local time of landing on the pad, counted on from `loaded_at`'s midnight); the HUD deadline is `hud().delivery_text()` (`MINE BY 14:30`, `MINE OVERDUE SINCE 14:30`) and `hud().delivery_overdue()` (red), and the HUD clock line is `hud().clock_text()` (`VALLEY  14:07`). `scene_test.gd` has the Transport offer helpers: `open_transport_offers()`, `select_row(i)`, `listed(prefix)`, `reason_for(offer)`, `offers()`, `known_offer(passengers, cargo, time_left)` (to the Valley; set its `destination` for another location), `open_details(offer)` (selects its row and opens it, leaving the cursor on the row), `accept(offer)` (opens it, Down to its ACCEPT, Return, Yes) and `start_loading(offers)` (puts the offers in the pool, accepts them, presses START LOADING, keeping the DESTINATION and START PERIOD the Office opened on, and waits for the Terminal to power off). The Terminal powers on (opening) and off (closing) with a ~0.3 s CRT effect, during which `office().powering` is true and Office input is ignored; `start()`, `choose_ship()` and `restart()` wait it out, and `wait_for_terminal()` does so elsewhere (`restart(false)` skips the wait). `dock_in_spot()` places the ship in the parking spot so it docks and waits past the 0.3 s leveling, so the "ENTER THE OFFICE?" popup is open: answer it (e.g. `press(&"ui_accept")` for the default No) before testing refuelling.
  - Tests extend `res://tests/scene_test.gd`. They drive the game with real input actions (`press`, `hold`, `release`), wait with `frames(n)` or `seconds(t)`, and check only what a player could observe: the lander's state, position, velocity, fuel, total mass and ship type, and the message text (`message()`, empty while hidden). `world()` is the World; `pad_x()` is the current location's pad centre x and `pad_visual_center_x()` the drawn pad's middle. `clock()` is the planet clock. `world().sky` is the current sky reading (`ground_color`, `darkness`, `star_brightness`), taken from the clock on the next physics frame after the time changes; `world().background_color(y)` is the gradient at a height. `world().shown_stars()` is every star drawn, as `World.Star`s (`position`, `radius`, `color`; fully transparent ones left out): the surface's night stars, then the deep-space stars. `world().shown_decor(camera_center, zoom)` is every decor piece drawn (the Backdrop's ridges, farther first, then the clouds, the vehicles' hulls, the Beacon, the Foreground and the city lights), as `World.Decor`s: `layer` (`World.DecorLayer`: `BACKDROP` for a location's ridges or skyline, `PARALLAX` for other decor behind the surface such as clouds and flying vehicles, `FOREGROUND`, `BEACON`, `LIGHTS` for city lights), `part` (`World.DecorPart`: `LIGHT`, `GLOW`, `RIDGE`, `CLOUD`, `SILHOUETTE`, `WINDOWS` (all of a location's windows, one mesh, as its bounding box), `NEON`, `WARNING_LIGHT`, `VEHICLE_LIGHT` or `VEHICLE`), `has_collider`, `color` (as drawn, tinted by its own and its ancestors' modulate; a gradient-filled ridge gives the average of its vertex colours), `scroll_scale` (its parallax layer's, `Vector2.ONE` outside any) and `screen_area` (its bounding `Rect2` on screen for a camera centred on `camera_center` at `zoom`, the viewport's centre being the camera's, with its parallax layer shifted for that camera). `tests/test_valley_depth.gd` has the Foreground fairness, darkness and view-coverage checks (`check_foreground_is_fair()`, `check_foreground_is_darkest()`, `check_layers_cover_the_view()`), written to run for any location. `ground()` and `mountain()` are the ground's and a mountain's visuals and `shown_color(polygon)` a Polygon2D's colour as drawn (tinted by its own and its ancestors' modulate), for checking dimming. A test may move to another location with `clock().move_to(location)`, e.g. `Location.MINE`, `Location.METROPOLIS` (its geometry constants `Location.ROOF_Y`, `STREET_Y`, `PAD_ROOF_WIDTH`, `PAD_BUILDING_LEFT`/`RIGHT`, `ANTENNA_TOP_Y`) or a `Location.new()` with its own pad centre (and, for a rooftop-like test location, its own `ground_y`, `ground_width` and `lowest_y`, or its own `backdrop` of `Backdrop.Ridge.new(top_edge, darkening)`s); the current location's terrain visuals are the `Polygon2D` children of `main().get_node("Terrain")`, read the current location's ground height with `Location.MINE.ground_y` (and the Mine's step with `Location.BENCH_STEP`), set the drift count directly with `clock().drift` (in-game minutes above the Border, e.g. `PlanetClock.DRIFT_MINUTES - 10.0` moves the location on after 5 real seconds above the Border with the surface out of view), set the local time directly with `clock().minutes` (in-game minutes since midnight; tests set half a minute past the hour they read, so whole-minute readings aren't on a boundary), and seed the clock with `clock().reseed(n)` (seeds its `rng` and starts the run over: the Valley, a new random Evening minute).
  - To set up a start position, call `place(position, velocity)`: it puts the lander Flying there through `fly_from()`, the same transition the game uses. For anything else, set the lander's public state directly. For Transport offers, a test may seed the Economy with `economy().reseed(n)` (a new pool of 5 from that seed, numbered from `#001`, with a fresh 30–90 s wait for the next offer) and put a known offer in the pool with `economy().put_offer(offer)` (it adds the offer, or replaces the one closest to expiring when 7 are listed, and takes the next number). Border crossing tests seed the Lander's disturbance with `lander().rng.seed = n`. `raise_to_maximum_weight()` sets a Load (`load_mass`) that brings a full ship to its Maximum weight. `restart()` presses R and waits for the reloaded scene; since R on the Surface only teleports to the pad, it first moves a ship that's below the border into deep space. `crash_in_deep_space()` crashes the ship into the station's underside.
  - Headless runs don't render anything, so visuals and game feel need a human playtest.
