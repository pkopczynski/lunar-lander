# Flight

How the ship flies, docks, lands and crashes, and what the HUD shows. The Office, missions and money are in [`office.md`](office.md); locations, the clock and scenery in [`world-and-scenery.md`](world-and-scenery.md). Terms are defined in [`GLOSSARY.md`](../../GLOSSARY.md).

Tuning values are constants at the top of each script: world layout in `world.gd`, station shape in `station.gd`, per-ship values in `ship_type.gd`, rules shared by every ship type in `lander.gd`, camera in `camera.gd`. Those files are the source of truth for the numbers.

- **Controls:** in flight, Left/Right rotate, Up thrusts along the lander's nose (both ignored for a moment after a border crossing, see Border), R after a crash or while Stranded is Recovery (see Crashed and Stranded); otherwise R does nothing, except in debug builds, where R on the Surface puts the ship back on the pad (see Debug pad shortcut). R never restarts. `restart` is a custom action bound by physical key. The Office's keys are in [`office.md`](office.md).
- **Camera:** follows the lander with smoothing and stays inside the world. Zoom is 1.0 at ≤ 50 px/s, easing out to 0.65 at ≥ 400 px/s.
- **Start:** once the player leaves the Office, the lander (the Sparrow unless another ship type was chosen) is Docked in the centre of the station's parking spot (re-picked at random on each fresh start), facing up, at rest, with a full tank. The pad is a destination only: fly out of the maze and down to the surface to land there.
- **Ship types:** each ship type has its own hull, flame, default dry mass, Maximum weight, Tank, passenger seats, cargo capacity, thrust, fuel burn and turn rate. Thrust and burn are given at the reference mass. The numbers are in `ship_type.gd` (`ShipType.SPARROW`, `HALO`, `MULE`). The Halo is a C-shaped ring open at the nose (concave hull, gap included in the collision shape), flame at the back; it climbs off the pad at ~80% of the Sparrow's rate, and a full tank lasts ~1.2× as long as the Sparrow's. The Mule is a box with its two nose corners rounded (back corners square), long axis along the thrust, flame on the short side at the back; it climbs off the pad at ~60% of the Sparrow's rate, and a full tank lasts ~1.4× as long as the Sparrow's. Each Maximum weight is the highest total at which that ship type still hovers and reaches the border crossing speed (`tests/test_*.gd` check this by adding a Load that brings a full ship to it). At full seats and cargo, every ship type has room for less than a full Tank. Each ship type's cargo capacity is its maximum capacity, the hard limit; its full-Tank cargo, the most cargo that still leaves room for a full Tank, is Maximum weight − dry mass − Tank − passengers × 80 kg, kept within 0 and the maximum capacity (`full_tank_cargo()`). Thrust-time targets are ratios to the Sparrow's measured full-tank thrust time (~24 s; its nominal Tank / burn is 20 s), not absolute seconds; `tests/test_halo.gd` and `tests/test_mule.gd` check them that way.
- **Mass:** total mass = dry mass (always the ship type's default) + fuel + Load (the passengers and cargo carried, 0 until loading finishes). Maximum weight caps the total: the fuel cap is min(Tank, Maximum weight − dry mass − Load), and refuelling, on the pad and at the station, stops there. The factor `w = total / 15 000 kg` divides thrust acceleration and scales gravity by half as much (`1 + 0.5·(w−1)`). Fuel burn is multiplied by its own factor, which counts the Load at half its mass: `(dry mass + fuel + 0.5·Load) / 15 000 kg`, so a loaded ship burns more gently than its weight alone would give, and a ship without a Load burns at `w`. Both factors change live as fuel burns or refills. Maximum weight is chosen so every ship type can still hover and cross the border.
- **Fuel:** thrust burns fuel (scaled by the burn factor above); a default full ship without a Load empties the tank in ~20 s of thrust. Burn is ×2 for the first 1 s after lifting off the pad (not after undocking), and up to ×2 more when thrusting against the motion (scaled by how directly, and by speed up to 150 px/s). At 0 fuel the engine doesn't fire.
- **Border:** gravity fades smoothly to zero over the 200 px below the border and is zero above it. Deep space flight is pure Newtonian: thrust is still divided by `w`, and there's no drag. Crossing the border either way needs ≥ 150 px/s vertical speed. A slower ship is sprung back to the side it came from. A real crossing shakes the screen for 2 s, starts steering until the ship is 150 px past the line, and disturbs the ship: for 0.6 s at 150 px/s down to 0.3 s at ≥ 400 px/s (by speed at the crossing), Left/Right and Up are ignored while the nose wobbles 4 times (each up to half the largest turn either way) and settles on a random lasting turn, either way, of up to 12° at 150 px/s down to 3° at ≥ 400 px/s (never under a third of that). The velocity turns with the nose, keeping its speed, so the path bends by the same lasting angle; nothing eases back. A ship sprung back by the border isn't disturbed. The numbers are the `DISTURB_*` constants in `lander.gd`.
- **Steering:** the game only damps the ship's speed and pushes it back; it never turns the ship. The nose stays where the player aimed it, and Left/Right and thrust work as in normal flight (apart from the border's disturbance). After a border crossing, speed is damped to 100 px/s.
- **Sides:** below the mountain tops, the mountains wall both sides; touching them is a hit (see Touching a solid). Above them, both sides are soft walls: within 150 px of an edge, sideways speed toward the edge is damped, the ship is pushed back inward, keeping its rotation, and it can never pass the edge.
- **Station:** a straight 100 px entry corridor leads from the station's bottom face into a vertical shaft. Three arms branch left and three right, like two back-to-back E letters. On each run, one of the six arm endings, picked at random, holds a chamber with the 120×136 px parking spot (cyan outline). Touching a station wall is a hit (see Touching a solid).
- **Docked** needs the whole ship inside the parking spot, at ≤ 40 px/s, while not thrusting. It then behaves like Landed: the ship zeroes velocity, eases upright, refuels after 2 s, and shows "Docked!". While docked, Left/Right turn the ship in place so it can face the way out. Up undocks it. The ship must leave the spot before it can dock again.
- **Judged on every touchdown:** the result is decided the moment the lander touches the ground. Nothing can save a bounce.
- **Landing bands:** a touchdown with the lander center within the pad is judged by three bands, each safe up to its first number and a crash above its second: falling speed 50 → 80 px/s, sideways speed 25 → 40 px/s, tilt from upright 15° → 25° (angle is wrapped, so any number of full spins doesn't count).
  - **Landed:** inside every safe limit.
  - **Hard landing:** inside a band and past none. It lands, delivers and unloads as usual, but the ship in service loses Condition in a straight line from 0% at the band's safe edge to 25% at its crash edge; the worst band counts, not their sum. The label shows `HARD LANDING −12%` in place of "Landed!" while the ship stays on the pad, and the HUD flashes the loss. A Hard landing that takes Condition to 0% is a crash.
  - **Crashed:** past any band.
- **Touching a solid** anywhere but the pad (the ground off the pad, such as the Mine's pit floor or the Metropolis's pad roof beside the pad; the mountains; a location's terrain: the Mine's benches and its crane, the Metropolis's buildings, antenna and street; the station's walls) is a hit, judged by its **impact speed**, the ship's speed into the surface: below 15 px/s nothing happens and the ship just settles; from 15 px/s up to the crash speed of 120 px/s it loses Condition in a straight line from 0% to 50%, and bounces off at 35% of its impact speed, keeping 80% of its speed along the surface, with the controls undisturbed; above 120 px/s it crashes. A hit that takes Condition to 0% is a crash, so a worn ship crashes from a smaller hit. The ship keeps its attitude on contact. On the ground it slides to a stop at 60 px/s² (not while thrusting). It counts as resting below 4 px/s, on the ground or wedged against a steep face. A ship resting off the pad stays Flying, can take off again, and delivers nothing. Resting tipped over (past 90° from upright) for 2 s is a crash. Condition has no effect on flight and never harms the Load. Crossing the Border does no damage, and docking keeps its single speed limit.
- **Stranded:** resting off the pad with an empty tank (not tipped over). Physics stops, the lander turns amber and the label shows "Stranded!" plus "Press R for recovery". It's settled at once like a crash (the recovery fee, and Failed contracts for any Contracts aboard; the Load is lost), but the ship keeps its Condition and isn't a Wreck.
- **Landed:** the lander turns green, zeroes its velocity, eases upright over 0.3 s and rests (no gravity, no rotation). After 2 s it refuels at half a Tank per 6 s up to the fuel cap, showing "Refueling…". Up lifts off at any time (except while unloading), cancelling refuelling. A ship too heavy to climb just settles back, which counts as another landing.
- **Crashed:** physics stops, the lander turns red, and the label shows "Crashed!" plus "Press R for recovery". The crash is settled at once: the ship in service becomes a Wreck (Condition 0%), the recovery fee (5% of its Ship type's price: €12 500 Sparrow, €20 000 Mule, €32 500 Halo) is a Transaction, and each Contract aboard a loaded mission becomes a Failed contract settled for 50% of its reward (one Transaction each; none on a free flight). The Load is lost.
- **Recovery:** R after a crash or while Stranded (anywhere, in any build) puts the ship Docked at the station, still in service, and opens the Office main screen, with it tagged WRECKED after a crash. Nothing reloads: the Bank account, Transactions, owned ships, offer pool, planet clock and location carry on. The Office can't be left until the ship in service can fly: repair it (on credit if need be) or put another owned ship in service. Recovery may take the balance below €0; there is no game over.
- **HUD:** right edge, a vertical fuel bar showing how full the current ship type's Tank is, with a brown, wavy, eased fill (frame flashes red when empty), "FUEL" above, total mass in kg below, and under that the ship in service's Condition, `CONDITION 87%`, with each loss flashing briefly in red under it (`−6%`). In the upper right, always (flying, Docked, Landed or Crashed, on the Surface or in deep space), the clock line: the current location's name and the local time, `VALLEY  14:07` (HH:MM, no seconds). Under it, while delivering, the destination and the flight deadline as a local time, `MINE BY 14:30` (the clock line above still shows the current location): the local time when loading finished plus the flight deadline × 2 in-game minutes per real second, wrapped at midnight, with no day mark (a next-day deadline reads `MINE BY 01:10`). Once the flight deadline passes it turns into a red `MINE OVERDUE SINCE 14:30`. Only the presentation is in local time: the deadline and penalties still count real seconds from the end of loading.
- **Fresh start:** only loading the page starts a new Career from the scene's initial state: the Office main screen with SPARROW 1, a €20 000 balance, a new Transport offer pool numbered from `#001`, the parking spot re-picked, and the planet clock at the Valley at a new random Evening minute. (A New career from the Title screen comes later.)
- **Debug pad shortcut:** in debug builds (a testing shortcut), R on the Surface (below the border, flying or Landed, not Crashed or Stranded) puts the ship Landed on the current location's pad, upright and at rest, keeping its ship type, dry mass, fuel and Load.
- There is no win state: the Bank account balance is the only measure of a Career, and a crash costs money but doesn't end it. You can fly between the station and the pad, docking and landing as often as you like.

Deliberately not built yet: a score or high-score table, HUD readouts (speed, altitude), terrain generation, sound, explosions, multiple levels.
