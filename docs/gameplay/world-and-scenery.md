# World and scenery

The world's layout, the locations, the planet clock and location drift, and everything drawn around the ship: the Backdrop, clouds, Foreground, city lights and sky. Flight is in [`flight.md`](flight.md); the Office and missions in [`office.md`](office.md). Terms are defined in [`GLOSSARY.md`](../../GLOSSARY.md).

- **World:** a 2000 px wide column. From the bottom: the ground, the surface (with gravity), the border ~2270 px above the pad, deep space (no gravity, ~2600 px tall), and the station, which spans the full width and forms the ceiling. The surface's sky, and how dark its ground and mountains look, follow the local time (see Sky); deep space is black at every hour. The surface shows the current location: the Valley (flat ground between the mountains, the pad in the middle) or the Mine, an open-pit mine cut into the ground: from the Valley's ground level, benches step down (60 px steps, three a side, wider on the left) to a 600 px pit floor 180 px below the Valley's ground, with the pad off-centre on the pit floor toward its right wall. The camera follows the surface down to the pit floor. The benches are solid: touching them crashes the ship, like the mountains. The Metropolis (rough shapes for now) is a city on a street 192 px below the Valley's ground: two tall towers on the left (the first against the left mountain), the pad's building right of centre with its roof at about mid-height (300 px above the street), a thin solid antenna on that roof's left corner, and two lower roofs stepping down on its right to the right mountain, the approach. Only a 140 px stretch of the pad building's roof around the pad is ground; touching down on the rest of that roof, any building, the antenna or the street crashes the ship. Gaps between buildings are at least ~2.5× the Mule's length (180 px and more). The camera follows down to the street. The mountains' outline and collision, deep space and station are shared; each location dresses the mountains in its own look (forested slopes under bare rocky tops in the Valley, rock cut into benches at the Mine, a wall of buildings at the Metropolis) and has its own Backdrop (the Valley distant ranges, Tatra-like peaks and forested hills, the Mine Himalaya-like peaks and its far walls, the Metropolis a skyline); the ground is too, at the location's height and width: by default it spans the whole world, but a location can narrow it to a landing surface centred on its pad (beside it the ship falls past its height onto whatever terrain is below, which crashes it). The camera's bottom limit, and the sky's ground colour filling below the Valley's ground, follow the location's lowest point (by default its ground's bottom, so the pit floor at the Mine). The location changes when a mission starts (see Loading in [`office.md`](office.md)) and by drifting (see Location drift); the planet clock owns it, and the World builds that location's pad, terrain and Backdrop each time it changes. The Mine's and the Metropolis's geometry is in `location.gd`.
- **Local time:** one planet clock, in in-game minutes, advancing smoothly at 120× real time: 1 real second is 2 in-game minutes, 30 s an hour, a whole day 12 real minutes; 23:59 wraps to 00:00. It runs whenever the world does (flying, Docked, loading, Landed, unloading, Crashed) and stops while the Office shows (it pauses with the tree). A run starts at the Valley at a random whole minute between 18:00 and 23:59 (the Evening start period). See `docs/adr/0002-one-planet-clock.md`.
- **Location drift:** the planet turns under the station. A hidden count adds in-game time while the ship is above the Border, in any state (flying, Docked, held, loading, Crashed). Going below the Border starts it over, so time on the surface never counts, and so does every location change. At 48 in-game hours (24 real minutes) the location becomes the next one in the location table (Valley → Mine → Metropolis → Valley…), in any phase, including during a delivery: landing on the new location's pad unloads the Load, but the Contracts pay €0 unless it's their destination. The change waits while any part of the surface (anything below the Border) is inside the camera's visible area, and happens on the first frame it isn't, so the surface never visibly swaps; the HUD name changes in the same frame as the pad and terrain. The local time carries on unchanged. The count stops while the Office shows (it pauses with the tree). See `docs/adr/0002-one-planet-clock.md`.
- **Backdrop:** each location's own scenery behind its surface, with no collision, built when the World shows that location. The Metropolis shows a dense skyline in four depths, each its own parallax layer, filled like the ridges: clusters of towers of varied heights with lower districts between them, their roofs flat, sloped, gabled, pagoda-like or crowned with spires and radio masts, and three monumental megastructures in the farthest depth rising far above it, the tallest about 1500 px above the pad right behind it, so some skyline stays in view as the ship climbs. Pale lit windows on the three nearest depths get fewer, smaller and dimmer with distance, always smaller and paler than the buildings' windows; the farthest depth and the megastructures carry only sparse red beacons; a band of haze lies behind each nearer depth, veiling the feet of the one behind it. The skyline's lights follow the city lights (faint by day, full at night, never dimmed). The Valley shows four depths: long, soft distant ranges; jagged High Tatra-like peaks towering above the mountain tops, tallest near Rysy, with an oddly thin needle on the right; a nearer, rounded ridge with Giewont's sleeping knight and spruce forest on its slopes; and, nearest, rolling hills, low and open around the pad, carrying giant sequoias (columns taller than any tree on Earth, one with a forked crown) among spruces toward the mountains' feet. The Mine shows four layers: tall, jagged Himalaya-like peaks towering above the mountain tops, lower sharp foothills, its upper walls (benches stacked high on both sides of the pit up to rims ~1000 px above the pad carrying factories, a tank, a processing plant and chimneys, with a headframe, a crane and a conveyor on the benches and an aerial ropeway across the sky between two towers; they rise with the camera more than the other layers, `UPPER_WALLS_SCROLL`, so the ship still feels inside the mine 1200 px above the pad), and its far walls, terraced down well below the benches so the pit looks deep, with a headframe, a chimney and two cranes on them; its layers move more slowly sideways than the Valley's (stronger parallax). Only the crane over the Mine's left benches is solid (terrain); the Backdrop's cranes have no collider. Signs of work show on the Mine's structures: red warning lights blinking a little out of step on its chimneys, ropeway towers, headframe and crane masts (never dimmed: faint by day, full at night, like the city's), and a few slow movers, each a silhouette in its depth's colour: a trolley with its hook running along the upper benches' crane jib, a hauler driving back and forth on the far walls' deep floor behind the pad, and lumps of ore riding up the conveyor. None has a collider, and no other location shows them. Each is filled with one colour taken from the sky behind the farthest ridge's highest peak, down to the location's lowest point: the farthest close to the sky, each nearer one darker (its darkening, such as `TATRA_FAR_DARKENING`, by day) and a little more saturated. Neighbouring depths always differ by a clear step in luminance (`Backdrop.MIN_STEP`), at every hour: at night the farther ridges and roofs are faintly moonlit, a little lighter than the dark sky, so the layers never merge into black, and the nearest stays clearly lighter than the Foreground. Each ridge is its own parallax layer, moving more slowly than the world, the farther one more so (such as `TATRA_FAR_SCROLL`), mostly sideways. Vertically the ground-rooted layers (ridges, skyline depths, far walls) stay planted on the ground but spread out: the Valley's from about 0.85 (the distant ranges) to 0.97 (the hills), the skyline's from 0.95 to 0.98, the Mine's from 0.94 to 0.975, with its upper walls at 0.85. They visibly part in the last ~500 px of a descent and the farther ones rise into the sky as the ship climbs, the Mine's upper walls most. **Vertical parallax through the whole sky:** about 90% of the flying around a pad is vertical, so the surface sky is filled up to the Border with decor, and up to ~1200 px above the pad something in view moves past the ship at a different vertical speed from what's behind it: the Moons and the far clouds are fixed in the world (scroll scale 1: playtests found them riding up with the ship looked wrong), so they stay in their spot in the sky and sink out of view as the ship climbs, long before the Border; then the Vehicles' lanes (the skyline's nearer depths, 0.962 to 0.982), the ground-rooted layers, and in front of the world the Foreground (1.06 to 1.07) and the wisps (1.12). The far clouds and the Moons are placed as seen from the pad and stay there on screen, revealed as the ridges sink during a climb; the wisps' heights are shares of each location's climb from its pad to the Border. `tests/test_whole_sky.gd` checks, at every location, by day and at night, that some decor is in view (standing out from the sky behind it by at least 0.1 in RGB, so clouds stay faintly moonlit at night) at every camera height from the pad until the Border comes into view, that what's in view spans two clearly different vertical scroll factors at the capture tool's heights up to 1200 px (higher up only the static sky may be in view), and that no decor of any kind is in view with the camera in deep space. Every parallax layer (Backdrop, clouds, flying vehicles, Foreground) is anchored on the pad's top: with the camera centred there it sits exactly where it was designed. The mountains, ground and terrain stay fixed in the world, and the mountains cover the ridges' ends. Since the ridges derive from the gradient, they follow the sky.
- **Solid vs decor** (a hard rule, so the player never mistakes what crashes them for scenery): solid terrain (the ground, the Mountains, a location's terrain: the Mine's benches and crane arm, the Metropolis's buildings, street and antenna) keeps its own opaque material colour (the Mountains' in `MountainLook`, `World.TERRAIN_COLOR`, the ground's in `main.tscn`), only dimmed by the sky's darkness, and stays clearly distinct from every Backdrop, parallax and Foreground piece at every hour. The Backdrop is always sky-tinted; the Foreground is near-black and darker than everything else (only translucent cloud wisps may differ; the Valley crowns' night glows, drawn in front with them, are listed as `LIGHTS`); decor windows are smaller and paler than the solid buildings' windows; no decor has a collider. `tests/test_tonal_depth.gd` checks the depth steps at the sky tests' hours and every solid colour (the ground, the Mountains, the terrain) against every decor piece every 15 minutes of the day, as `tests/test_mountains.gd` does for the Mountains alone, since the sky-tinted decor passes through grey and brown between those hours: dark neutral or mid-brown solids can't stay clear of it, so the solid colours are light or clearly saturated.
- **Clouds:** every location has clouds in three depths, each with its own vertical scroll factor and wind: thin, fibrous cirrus streaks high in the sky and heaped cumulus lower, partly behind the scenery (both filling the sky from just above the pad to just below the Border, fixed in the world, so they stay in their spot in the sky as the ship flies around; they drift slowly sideways on the wind), and near translucent wisps in the Foreground that pass in front of the ship at altitude. White in the Valley, grey smog in the Metropolis, dust in the Mine, the smog and dust a little flatter. They drift rightwards on the wind by themselves and wrap around from one side to the other out of sight; a wisp never enters the Approach corridor (it fades out toward the corridor and wraps there unseen). None shows above the Border. Each takes the sky's colour at its height and the hour's sky colour, lightened by day and darkened with the sky at night (yellow at dawn, orange in the evening). No collider.
- **Foreground:** silhouettes in front of the ship, moving faster than the world, which can briefly hide the ship and its path (Hollow Knight style): at the Valley giant sequoias, alone and in groups, among spruces, broad trees, shrubs and a few oddly shaped alien crowns, rising from one continuous near ground on each side of the open clearing around the pad (both ends of each dip below the lowest point, so no side shows), the tallest crowns reaching about 1200 px above the pad and passing in front of the ship well above it; at the Mine, moving faster still (stronger parallax), two continuous near strips of spoil, one on each side of the corridor, running out of view on their outer ends and dropping steeply below the view toward the corridor so no block side shows: on the left a spoil heap and a haul truck on the left benches, then an ore heap on the pit floor with a stacker's tower; on the right an ore heap under a conveyor's head tower, its gallery sloping down onto the heap; both towers top out just below the crane's arm, so they never hide it yet stay in view on each side of the corridor through the last ~500 px of the approach; at the Metropolis near rooftops rising from one continuous low parapet with pipes (which passes under the corridor, below the pad's roof on screen): on the left a roof with a water tank and antennas and a pagoda-like tower reaching far above the pad, on the right a tower with a sloped roof and antennas, then a roof with a billboard and a water tank; the two towers stand just beside the corridor, so they stay in view through the landing on the pad's roof. Opaque and near-black, darker than all other scenery at every hour and a little darker at night, with no collider and no lights but a faint glow in some Valley crowns at night, fading in with the night like the other lights. **Approach corridor:** the column above the pad, 40 px wider than the pad on each side, which no Foreground piece covers at any camera height from the pad up to the Border, at any zoom, so the way down to the pad always stays in sight. With the camera following the ship within ~250 px above the pad, no Foreground piece covers the pad, the ship or the edge of solid terrain there either. Outside the corridor, each location keeps at least one Foreground piece in view on each side of it through the last ~500 px of the approach (all three do; `BESIDE_PENDING` in `tests/test_approach_corridor.gd` lists any location that doesn't yet, and a location comes off it once its Foreground does). A new location's Foreground must keep these rules. The clouds' wisps and the Valley crowns' night glows are the only translucent Foreground pieces; they keep these rules too.
- **City lights:** the Metropolis's buildings have lit windows, neon signs and red warning lights blinking on the towers' and the lower roof's corners and the antenna's tip, and its Vehicles fly in lanes at several heights and depths of its sky behind the buildings, up to about two-thirds of the way to the Border (`Location.METROPOLIS_LANES`), each with a light and a fading trail of light, wrapping around out of sight. The lights are never dimmed: faint by day, they grow brighter as the sky darkens, full at night, so the city reads at a glance in the dark. The buildings and the vehicles' hulls dim with the scenery. None of it has a collider.
- **Sky:** the sky follows the local time. Its ground-level colour blends smoothly (no jumps at hour boundaries, wrapping around midnight) between keyframes: 03:00 dark navy, 07:30 pale yellow, 13:00 blue, 19:00 today's surface orange (exactly), 23:00 dark navy (so it stays navy through midnight). The gradient still fades from it to black at the Border, so deep space looks the same at every hour. The ground, mountains and a location's terrain (the Mine's benches) are darkened by the sky's darkness, most at night and not at all by day or at 19:00; the pad never is, so it stays easy to find. The pad's Beacon, a small light on each of its edges, pulses slowly (never going out) and is never dimmed either; its soft glow is faint by day and grows with the sky's darkness, strongest at night. It has no collider: the ship flies through it. Faint night stars cover the whole surface sky, from the Border down to the ground, drawn behind the Backdrop (the ridges, ground and mountains cover them) at the sky's star brightness: none by day, fading in 20:30–22:30 and out 05:00–07:00, capped faint. The deep-space stars and their fade just below the Border are the same at every hour. Time changes nothing in gameplay. The keyframes are constants in `sky_look.gd`.
